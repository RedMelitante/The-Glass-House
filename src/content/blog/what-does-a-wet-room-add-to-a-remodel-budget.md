A curbless wet room typically adds $2,000 to $5,000 over a comparable curbed shower enclosure driven by four specific line items, not a single blanket "wet room upcharge." Here's exactly where that money goes.

> 📖Planning the layout itself? Start with [**Curbless Walk-In Showers & Wet Rooms: Planning Slope, Drainage & Fixed Glass Layout**.](/curbless-walk-in-showers-wet-rooms-planning-slope-drainage-fixed-glass-layout/)

Once a homeowner decides a curbless wet room is the right direction for their bathroom remodel, the next question is inevitably about money. Unlike a standard shower pan, a wet room touches four distinct trades waterproofing, drainage, floor framing, and glass and each one carries its own incremental cost versus a conventional curbed enclosure. Understanding where that money actually goes is more useful than a single "wet rooms cost X% more" headline number.

> 💵A Note on These Numbers
>
> The figures on this page are general industry ranges for custom bathroom remodeling, intended for budgeting and comparison not a quote. Your actual price depends on your specific contractor, existing floor structure, and site conditions. [Book a free consultation](/get-free-estimate/) for an exact number on your project.

## Waterproofing Labor Cost vs. a Standard Shower Pan

A standard shower installs on a pre-formed pan or a mud-set pan with a contained CPE/PVC liner, waterproofing a relatively small, boxed-in footprint. A wet room requires a continuous waterproof membrane sheet or liquid-applied, as covered in our [waterproofing membrane guide](/wet-room-waterproofing-membrane-systems-explained/) across the entire wet area floor, not just the shower footprint.

- **Standard shower pan waterproofing labor:** Typically $400–$800
- **Full wet room floor waterproofing labor:** Typically $1,200–$2,200
- **Typical premium:** +$700–$1,500

The larger surface area, the precision required at the drain connection, and the flood-testing step all add labor time that a contained shower pan doesn't require.

---

## Linear Drain Installation Cost vs. Point Drain

Wet rooms are frequently paired with a linear drain, since a single-direction slope to a linear drain is often easier to achieve across an open floor than a four-way slope to a central point drain though a point drain can work in a wet room too, and a linear drain can be used in a standard shower as well. The cost difference comes down to the fixture and the slope precision it requires, not the room type itself.

- **Point drain (fixture + install):** Typically $150–$300
- **Linear drain (fixture + install):** Typically $400–$900
- **Typical premium:** +$250–$600

## Slope Framing & Floor Build-Up Cost

This is usually the largest single line item, and the one most dependent on whether you're building new or retrofitting. A curbed shower contains its slope entirely within the pan itself, requiring minimal changes to the surrounding floor structure. A curbless wet room needs the slope-to-drain built into the actual floor framing often by recessing joists near the drain or building up the surrounding floor height to meet a lowered shower zone.

- **New construction (framing planned from the start):** Typically $800–$1,500 in additional framing and subfloor work
- **Retrofit into an existing floor structure:** Typically $1,500–$2,500, reflecting the added labor of modifying framing that's already in place

This is also the item most likely to expand once a contractor opens the floor and finds a condition that wasn't visible beforehand budgeting toward the higher end of the range is the more conservative approach for a retrofit.

## Fixed Glass Panel Pricing vs. a Framed Door Alternative

Wet rooms commonly use one or more large fixed glass panels rather than a swinging or sliding door, since there's no curb or track to anchor a door to. A fixed panel is typically wall-clamp mounted rather than hinged, and because it's often a single larger span of glass, per-square-foot pricing runs comparable to or slightly above a [standard frameless hinged door](/shower-door-configurations-compared-the-4-types-of-shower-doors/) while a basic framed door alternative remains the lowest-cost glass option, at the cost of a visible metal frame and channel.

| Glass Configuration | Typical Installed Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framed swinging door | $40–$60/sq ft | Lowest cost, visible frame |
| Frameless hinged door | $65–$85/sq ft | Standard frameless baseline, 3/8″ |
| Fixed wet room panel | $70–$95/sq ft | Wall-clamp mounted, often a larger single span |

If low-iron glass is also being specified for the fixed panel a common pairing, since a large fixed span is exactly where iron tint is most visible see our [low-iron cost breakdown](/does-low-iron-glass-cost-more-a-real-price-breakdown/) for that additional premium on top of the base glass pricing above.

## Total Premium Range vs. a Comparable Curbed Enclosure

Adding up the four components above against a baseline curbed frameless enclosure:

| Line Item | Typical Added Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Waterproofing | +$700–$1,500 |
| Drain (if upgrading to linear) | +$250–$600 |
| Slope framing / floor build-up | +$800–$2,500 |
| Glass (fixed panel vs. hinged door, per typical sq ft) | +$100–$400 |
|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Typical Total Premium: $2,000–$5,000 | |

Against a baseline curbed frameless enclosure project of roughly $4,500–$9,000, this places a comparable wet room project in the general range of $6,500–$14,000 with the floor framing work, particularly in a retrofit, being the single biggest driver of where a given project lands within that range.
